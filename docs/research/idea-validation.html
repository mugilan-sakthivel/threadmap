Live voice → visual notes for podcasters

Idea validation · researched 25 Oct 2026 · Mic → live transcript → LLM extracts topics, claims, and follow-ups → draws them on an Excalidraw canvas as the conversation happens
Verdict: worth building as a personal project. As a business it's only worth it with a narrower audience.
Nobody ships this exact product yet, so there is a real gap. Every building block is cheap and commodity, though, so the gap can close fast. The weak spot is the buyer. Audio-only podcasters mostly pay for post-production (show notes, clips), and their listeners never see a live diagram. The value shows up wherever the visual is seen: video podcasts and livestreams (an on-screen "visual co-host") and live events/webinars, where people pay human graphic recorders $1–2.5k a day.

3. How much is it worth? (honest ranges)

  • Indie ceiling in podcasting is proven but modest. Podsqueeze reached about $6.5k MRR in 2 months and about $16k MRR later, with roughly 20% monthly churn. Castmagic claims $0 → $1M ARR in its first year.
  • Rough math: 480k active shows × ~15% that pay for tools × 1% capture ≈ 720 customers × $25 ≈ $18k MRR (~$215k ARR). That's a strong indie result, not a venture outcome.
  • Upside case: become the "AI graphic recorder" for events and webinars (B2B, $100s per event). This is where the bigger money is, and it's the same tech.
  • Unit economics are good: STT costs about $0.15–0.45/hr, LLM extraction maybe $0.20–1/hr. At $25/mo for about 10 hrs, gross margin is around 70–90%.
  • Acquisition value: a working live-visual layer is a natural feature for Riverside, StreamYard, Restream, Otter, or Miro. That's a plausible exit for a small team.

4. Risks that could kill it

  • Thin moat: STT, LLMs, and Excalidraw are all off-the-shelf, and Otter or Riverside could ship this in a quarter. Your edge has to be quality of the live visual (stable layout, no jitter, good taste) plus speed.
  • The diagram is a distraction for the host. A host shouldn't be watching a screen mid-interview. Design it for the audience or for after the show, not as a host co-pilot.
  • Live layout is the hard problem. Redrawing the whole canvas every 30s looks chaotic. You need incremental, append-only placement (new nodes attach to existing ones).
  • Latency and accuracy: a wrong claim drawn live on a public stream is embarrassing. Add a short buffer (10–20s) or host approval.
  • Consent and recording laws apply to guests (two-party consent states and countries).

5. Tech note: you don't need MCP for the product

An MCP server is the right tool for prototyping inside Claude Desktop/Code. For a real-time app, call things directly: streaming STT (AssemblyAI/Deepgram WebSocket) → an LLM every N seconds returns structured diff ops (add node, link, highlight) → render with @excalidraw/excalidraw (or @excalidraw/mermaid-to-excalidraw) in the browser. Optional: an OBS browser source so streamers can put it on screen.

6. Recommended wedge and 2-week validation plan

Position it as a "live visual co-host for video podcasts and streams." It draws the conversation map on screen as you talk, and after the show you get a shareable visual episode map (carousel or thumbnail-ready).

  1. Days 1–5: build a web prototype with mic in, live Excalidraw canvas, and an OBS browser-source URL.
  2. Day 6: run it over 3 well-known podcast clips and record a 60s side-by-side demo. Post it on X, Reddit (r/podcasting, r/NewTubers), and LinkedIn.
  3. Days 7–10: landing page with a waitlist and a "$9 founding member" pre-order button. DM 30 video podcasters and 10 event/webinar organizers.
  4. Days 11–14: let 5 creators use it on a real episode. Measure whether they put it on screen and whether they share the diagram.
