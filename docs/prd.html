ThreadMap: live visual notes for podcasters

Product Requirements Document · v1.0 · 26 Oct 2026 · Owner: Mugilan S · Status: MVP built

One line: ThreadMap listens to a podcast or interview as it happens and draws it on a canvas: every question becomes a section, and spoken structures (a layered framework, a process, a funnel, a feedback loop, a comparison) become real diagrams that fill in as the guest explains them. A live transcript and running notes (key points, quotes, follow-up questions) log on the right. When the episode ends, the host has a visual map, show notes and follow-ups without having taken a single note.

1. Problem

  • Hosts take notes while they talk. To keep track of what was asked, what the guest said and what to follow up on, interview hosts scribble notes mid-conversation. That splits their attention and the notes are messy and incomplete.
  • Today's AI tools work after the fact. Riverside, Castmagic and Descript generate show notes after recording. Otter and Granola are live but produce plain text. None turn a live conversation into a structured visual.
  • Indian creators code-switch. Hosts speak Tamil/Hindi mixed with English (Tanglish/Hinglish), which English-first tools handle poorly.
  • Human graphic recorders do exactly this for events, at $1,000–2,500 per day, so the value is proven but out of reach for most creators.

3. Goals and non-goals

Goals (MVP)

  1. Words → diagram on screen in a few seconds (target ≤ 4 s with a hosted fast model).
  2. Choose the right diagram from meaning, not keywords: a pyramid is drawn even if nobody says “pyramid”.
  3. Readable board for a 1–2 hour episode: sections flow top to bottom, then into new columns.
  4. Work for mixed Tamil/Hindi + English speech; draw the map in the host's chosen language.
  5. Never interrupt the host: glanceable, calm motion, no required interaction during recording.

Non-goals (MVP)

  • Recording or hosting the audio/video itself (Riverside/Zoom already do this).
  • Editing, clipping or publishing the episode.
  • Accounts, billing, teams and audience overlays (planned, see Milestones).

6. How the shape is chosen (core intelligence)

The model decides the template from the relationship between items, never from trigger words:

RelationshipTemplate
Each item needs the one before it (“without X nothing works”, “only once that's solid…”)Pyramid (most basic at the bottom)
Order matters (a process or a dated story)Steps / Timeline
Each stage has fewer people than the lastFunnel
The last item leads back to the firstCycle
Two sides set against each otherComparison (with named sides)
Independent items of the same kindGrid
None of the aboveList
  • Commit late, correct later: unclear → start a list; convert once the relationship is clear (labels kept); re-check the previous section when the topic moves on; no flip-flopping.
  • Draw only substance: frameworks, processes, advice, real comparisons. Banter and tangents get at most a note.
  • Explainable: every diagram carries a short “why” (e.g. “three actions in a required sequence”).

InternationalisationGrapheme-aware text wrapping for Tamil/Devanagari; map language independent of spoken language
QualityTypecheck, lint, unit tests and build on every PR (CI); live-model shape eval gated at 80%
